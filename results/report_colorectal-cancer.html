KDR · colorectal cancer

On KDR, the best signature reaches enrichment 2.87× at the top quartile against an arithmetic maximum of 4.10× (70% of achievable, n = 41) — and that signature is the free P2Rank pocket geometry, which beats the 86-credit BoltzGen design consensus (2.05×). The design step is not what is doing the work.

Target KDR (UniProt P35968), structure 3VHE. n = 41 drugs scored of 42 co-folded: 10 known binders · 12 hard decoys · 19 decoys. Ranked by precision_in_core against the P2Rank pocket geometry.

These are computational hypotheses requiring experimental validation.

Nothing on this page is a measurement. Every drug on this board was placed there by a structure-prediction model co-folding it into a predicted pocket, then ranked by how much of a predicted residue set it overlaps. No affinity, Kd, IC50 or potency is predicted, read, or implied anywhere in this pipeline — Boltz-2 can emit an affinity score and the pipeline deliberately does not read it (PROJECT_GOAL.md 4.4). A high rank here is a reason to run an assay. It is not evidence that a drug binds, works, or treats anything.

Boltz-2 scores BoltzGen designs and the two share training data, and co-folding an approved drug against its known target means the model has likely seen that complex. The decoy arms exist because of that, and they are what make any number here interpretable.

Read the top of this board with the hard decoys in mind.

The highest-ranked hard decoys are lapatinib at 2, pemigatinib at 8 and bosutinib at 11 of 41 — approved kinase inhibitors carrying no annotation against KDR. 2 of them fall inside the top quartile that enrichment is measured over, and 8 of 12 outrank the lowest-placed known binder (rank 22).

This report cannot tell you whether that is a false positive or a gap in the annotation. These are promiscuous kinase inhibitors and the annotation tables used to assign roles are known to be incomplete, so a high score here is equally consistent with the pipeline being right and the label being wrong. Deciding between those two cases needs an assay, not another model. Every enrichment number on this page counts them as negatives, which is the conservative choice and may understate the ranking.

What this does and does not show. Raw ranks out of 41 and out of 4,099 are not on the same scale, so the percentile columns sit next to them and the gap is computed on percentiles rather than on the raw difference. Read that way, the two rankings are closer than the raw ranks suggest: the interface board wins on percentile for 4 of 10 drugs and loses on 6. The defensible claim is the narrow one: engaging the same site and resembling the reference ligand are different properties, and the chemical ranking places 8 of these 10 known binders beyond rank 400 of 4,099 — deep enough that a similarity screen would not reach them.

Coverage, checked not assumed. 10 of the 42 drugs on the board carry a chemical rank, and all 10 are known binders. The other 32 — every decoy and every hard decoy — are absent from candidates.json, which stores only the top 25 of the corpus plus the 32 known binders, not the full 4,099-drug ordering. Their chemical ranks were not computed anywhere in this run, so they are omitted rather than guessed at. This table is therefore a comparison over known binders only, and it cannot say how the two rankings treat a negative.

5 · What does not work

These are the run's negative results, kept here with their numbers because a reader deciding whether to trust this pipeline needs them more than the board above.

  1. The free pocket finder beats the BoltzGen signature in the product, not just in the ablation.

    Ranking these 41 drugs by the P2Rank rank-1 pocket gives enrichment 2.87× at the top quartile and a median known-binder rank of 8.0. The BoltzGen design consensus gives 2.05× and 12.5. The design step cost 85.54 credits and 29 minutes; P2Rank cost 0.47 s per structure (mean over the 1,531-structure batch run, 12 threads, 720.9 s wall). The board above is ranked by P2Rank pocket geometry for that reason.

    Spearman rho between the two rankings is 0.776 (p = 2.5e-09, n = 41) on precision_in_core. On the earlier, easier board it was 0.907 (n = 29), with both signatures returning identical enrichment there. Adding the hard decoys pulls the two rankings apart, and it pulls them apart against the design signature.

    This is ablation I6.1 (PROJECT_GOAL.md 8.3) appearing for the third time. It already held on KDR (Jaccard Δ = -0.324, 95% CI [-0.353, -0.294], Wilcoxon p = 7.3e-12, Holm-adjusted p = 0.0016, n = 38 structures) and on CDK2 (Δ = -0.155, 95% CI [-0.235, -0.084], Wilcoxon p = 0.00035, Holm-adjusted p = 0.0016, n = 31 structures). The design consensus does beat a random residue set of the same size on both targets (Jaccard Δ = 0.271 and 0.305), so it carries real information — it is simply worse than the free baseline, on every target measured so far.

  2. The known-ligand signature ranks worse than chance.

    Taking the contacts of the ligand in the holo structure as the signature gives enrichment 0.82×, below the 1.00× of an arbitrary ordering, with a median known-binder rank of 16.5 of 41. It is the only arm here that is actively misleading, and it is the arm with privileged information.

    One holo ligand's contact set describes that ligand, not the site. It is 23 residues shaped around a single chemotype, so scoring other drugs against it rewards resembling that one molecule's footprint rather than occupying the pocket. It is kept on this page as a reference arm and a warning, and it is not used to rank anything.

  3. Enrichment drops once the null gets harder, and the easy number should never be quoted on its own.

    An earlier run of this board scored the same drugs against the easy decoys only (29 drugs, 10 known binders, maximum 2.90×). This run adds 12 hard decoys — approved kinase inhibitors not annotated against KDR — raising n to 41 and the maximum to 4.10×. Because the raw enrichment ceiling moves with the base rate, the comparable quantity is the fraction of the achievable maximum, and it falls for every signature.

  4. Hard decoys score near the top, and this run cannot say whether that is an error.

    The highest-placed hard decoys are lapatinib at 2, pemigatinib at 8 and bosutinib at 11 of 41. 2 hard decoys fall inside the top quartile enrichment is measured over, and 8 of 12 outrank the lowest-placed known binder (rank 22) — the two populations interleave rather than separate. Counted as negatives, as every number on this page counts them, they are what pulls enrichment down from the easy-decoy figure.

    But the label is an annotation, not an observation. These are promiscuous approved kinase inhibitors, the annotation tables used to assign roles are incomplete, and nothing in this pipeline distinguishes “the ranking is wrong” from “the label is wrong”. The honest position is that the hard-decoy arm bounds the ranking from below, and that resolving these specific drugs needs an assay. See the note at the top of the board.

  5. The top-ranked drug on the board is not a known binder.

    Rank 1 is proxibarbal, decoy, at precision_in_core = 0.923. It reaches that by engaging only 13 residues, 12 of them in the core: a small ligand that sits wholly inside the pocket scores well on a precision metric without covering much of it. Its core coverage is 0.706, and it still misses 5 core residues.

    The metric is size-normalised on purpose — PROJECT_GOAL.md 1.4a warns that raw overlap ranks bigger ligands first for reasons unrelated to biology — and this is the cost of that choice, visible at rank 1. All five scores are stored per drug in the board JSON so the choice can be re-examined; the run records that enrichment is unchanged under any of them.

  6. Every design falls far below the confidence filter the plan proposed.

    The 24 designs this signature is built from span ipTM 0.119 to 0.420 (median 0.210). PROJECT_GOAL.md suggests filtering at 0.85; 0 of 24 reach it. The signature is a consensus over designs the project's own criterion would have discarded. It is reported that way rather than re-filtered until something survived, and it is one plausible reason the design arm underperforms the pocket finder above.
